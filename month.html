TRIBUTE TO JOSEF ALBERS

Inspired by Josef Albers's Homage to the Square (1950 to 1976). Albers arrived at the Bauhaus as a student and left it as a master, the first to make that jump. He ran the preliminary course after Itten and the glass workshop, and drew a stencil alphabet in 1926 that built every letter from a few squares and quarter-circles. From 1950 he painted the same nested squares more than a thousand times, never centred but pushed low, so that colour and not shape carried the picture. This page runs on that idea: one yellow, several grounds, and Futura's geometry set small, the way the Bauhaus wrote everything.

Fun fact: Albers painted every Homage with a palette knife, straight from the tube and unmixed, then wrote the paint makers on the back of the panel so anyone could check his recipe. Interaction of Color opens with the whole lesson in one line: a colour is almost never seen as it really is.
