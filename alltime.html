TRIBUTE TO LÁSZLÓ MOHOLY-NAGY

Inspired by László Moholy-Nagy's constructivist paintings and typophoto (1920s). Moholy-Nagy ran the metal workshop and, with Albers, the preliminary course. He made photograms without a camera, laid out the Bauhaus books, and painted crossed bars and circles that read like diagrams of motion. This page is the record laid out his way. One grid, plain grotesque type, and colour used as a signal rather than decoration.

Fun fact: in 1922 he said he ordered three enamel paintings from a sign factory by telephone, dictating the shapes from graph paper and the colours from the factory's chart. The paintings exist. Whether the phone call happened is still argued about.
