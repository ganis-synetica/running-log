TRIBUTE TO HERBERT BAYER

Inspired by Herbert Bayer's Universal alphabet (1925). Bayer drew it from circles and straight lines, in one case only. He put the reason on the Bauhaus letterhead he designed the same year: we write everything small, it saves time. This page follows him. The type is geometric and lowercase, and the three forms behind it are the Bauhaus set as the school taught it, yellow triangle, red square, blue circle.

Fun fact: Bayer's first printed work was money. In 1923, with German inflation at its worst, the state of Thuringia had the Bauhaus student design its emergency banknotes. He set them in plain sans type, with the values running to millions of marks.
